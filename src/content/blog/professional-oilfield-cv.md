This article focuses on *what to put in* an oilfield CV — how to present certifications and
experience honestly and clearly. If you specifically want formatting and keyword guidance for
passing automated applicant tracking systems, see our companion guide,
[How to Write an Oil & Gas CV That Passes ATS Screening](/blog/gulf-oil-gas-cv-tips), instead.

## Clarity matters more than design

An oilfield CV is often read first by an applicant tracking system and then by a recruiter, so
a clear, simply structured document tends to communicate better than a heavily designed one.
Workday's administrator documentation, for example, notes that resume parsing results vary with
the resume's format and recommends resumes without images or image-based styles.

## A practical structure to consider

- **Contact and summary** — a short, factual summary of your role focus and experience level, not a sales pitch.
- **Certifications** — list each certification by its exact, real name, and only certifications you currently and genuinely hold. Avoid listing an expired certificate as if it were current. See our [BOSIET and HUET: Certification Guide](/blog/bosiet-huet-certification-guide) for how these two specific certifications relate to each other.
- **Experience** — list roles with real dates, real employer names, and a factual description of responsibilities, in reverse chronological order.
- **Education and training** — include relevant formal education and completed training courses separately from certifications that require periodic renewal.

## On certifications specifically

Many offshore safety certificates expire. For example, OPITO lists a validity of 4 years for
HUET with Emergency Breathing System, and a maximum interval of 4 years between BOSIET (with EBS)
and its FOET refresher. If a certification has an expiry or refresher requirement, state its
status honestly — including the expiry date — rather than presenting it as unconditionally
current.

In Jobnura's editorial view, misrepresenting a safety certification's status is both a
professional-honesty issue and, in oilfield roles, a safety issue.

## Avoiding common CV mistakes

- Do not invent job titles or responsibilities you did not actually hold.
- Do not round up limited exposure to a skill into a full claim of expertise.
- Keep contact information accurate and current so a genuine opportunity can actually reach you.
