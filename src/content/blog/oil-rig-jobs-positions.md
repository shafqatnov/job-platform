## Rig crews are organized by responsibility, not just seniority

An oil rig crew is generally structured around a division of responsibility — drilling
operations, equipment maintenance, and site leadership — rather than a single career ladder.
Exact job titles, responsibilities, and reporting structures can vary between drilling
contractors and regions, so the descriptions below are general patterns, not a universal
standard. *(Needs verification: any specific employer's own title structure.)*

## Common position categories

The Driller, Derrickman and Floorman (Roughneck) descriptions below are paraphrased from
definitions listed in the IADC Lexicon, which draws them from the API's recommended practice on
occupational safety for drilling and well servicing (API RP 54).

- **Roustabout** — commonly described as an entry-level position involving general rig maintenance and manual labour under supervision. *(Needs verification: the IADC Lexicon has no entry for this title.)*
- **Roughneck (Floorman)** — a rig-crew member whose work station during hoisting is on the rig floor, who also carries out other operating and maintenance duties as directed by the supervisor. The role is also called rotary helper or driller's helper.
- **Derrickman** — a crew member whose work station is usually up in the derrick while pipe or rods are being hoisted into or lowered out of the hole.
- **Driller** — the crew's first-line supervisor, responsible for operating the drilling and hoisting equipment and for directing and training the crew.
- **Toolpusher** — commonly described as the senior rig supervisor overseeing drilling operations and crew coordination on behalf of the drilling contractor. *(Needs verification: the IADC Lexicon has no entry for this title.)*
