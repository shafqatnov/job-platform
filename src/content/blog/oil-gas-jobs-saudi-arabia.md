## A large, long-established oil & gas sector

Saudi Arabia's oil & gas sector is anchored by Aramco, the country's national oil company.
Aramco describes itself as "one of the world's largest integrated energy and chemicals
companies" and states that it manages Saudi Arabia's hydrocarbon reserves base.

The sector hires both Saudi nationals and international workers — Aramco, for example, runs a
dedicated hiring process for international applicants. The scale of current foreign hiring, by
role and by employer, changes over time and is not something this article attempts to quantify.
*(Needs verification: any current hiring-volume claim — this article intentionally makes none.)*

## General factors foreign candidates commonly need to plan for

- **Work visa and permit** — in Saudi Arabia the employer (the "establishment") applies for work visas through the Qiwa platform, according to the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD). The ministry also states that the employer must issue a work permit to an expatriate employee within the first 90 days after arrival in the Kingdom, and that the residence permit is handled through the Passports authority. Aramco similarly tells international hires that they will need a visa and a residence permit from the Saudi government, and that it supports them through the process. *(Needs verification: current documents and timelines — confirm with the employer and official authorities.)*
- **Relevant technical/safety certifications** — many technical and offshore-adjacent roles require recognized safety or technical certifications, similar to other Gulf markets — see our [BOSIET and HUET: Certification Guide](/blog/bosiet-huet-certification-guide). OPITO-approved centres in Al Khobar deliver HUET training. Specific requirements vary by role and employer.
- **Medical and background checks** — Aramco states that international hires must clear a medical examination and background checks during relocation.
- **Contract structure and rotation** — some roles, especially remote or site-based ones, are structured around rotational schedules rather than a standard five-day office week. Exact rotation patterns vary by employer and role. *(Needs verification: any specific rotation pattern for Saudi roles — this article does not claim a standard one exists across the industry.)*

## Practical guidance for candidates

- Verify any claimed visa-sponsorship offer directly with the employer rather than assuming it from a listing alone.
- Treat any request for upfront payment in exchange for a job offer, visa, or sponsorship as a serious warning sign. *(Needs verification: an official Saudi source on recruitment fraud to cite here.)*
- Confirm which safety/technical certifications a specific role requires before assuming a certificate you hold already satisfies it.
