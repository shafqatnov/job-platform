## Why ATS screening matters

Many large oil and gas employers receive applications through an applicant tracking system
(ATS) — their official career sites run on platforms such as Workday, SAP SuccessFactors and
Oracle Recruiting. When you upload a CV, these systems typically read ("parse") it to fill in
your application.

Layout affects how well that works. Workday's own administrator documentation states that
resume parsing results "can vary based on resume format and order of words", and recommends
resumes without images or image-based styles for best results. A plain, clearly structured CV
is therefore a safer choice than a heavily designed, image-based layout. *(Needs verification:
how other ATS products behave — this article only cites Workday's documentation.)*

## General structure guidance

- Use standard section headings (Experience, Certifications, Education) rather than invented ones.
- Avoid images, text boxes and image-based layouts for important information such as your name, job titles and certificates.
- List certifications by their real, exact name — this helps keyword matching and avoids ambiguity. See our [Certifications & Training](/blog/category/certifications-training) category for how to describe common certifications like BOSIET and HUET accurately.
- Keep safety/compliance certifications in their own clearly labeled section so they are easy to find.
